# Safe plates: diets, allergies, labels and the table clock

## Two kinds of restriction

| | Diet (`diet`) | Allergy (`allergy`) |
|---|---|---|
| Rules out dishes that | contain the item | contain it **or may contain it** |
| Unlabelled dish | UNKNOWN = unsafe | UNKNOWN = unsafe |
| Set-aside plate | only if one safe choice | always |
| Who confirms | the guest | the guest or guardian, directly |

## Diet keywords and what they exclude

| Diet | Excludes flags |
|---|---|
| vegetarian | beef, pork, poultry, lamb, gelatin, fish, shellfish, mollusc |
| vegan | all of the above + egg, dairy, honey |
| pescatarian | beef, pork, poultry, lamb, gelatin |
| halal | pork, alcohol; any beef/lamb/poultry must be marked `halal_meat` |
| no-pork / no-beef | pork / beef |
| gluten-free | gluten |
| dairy-free | dairy |
| no-alcohol | alcohol (including in sauces and desserts) |

If a guest's need does not fit a keyword (kosher, Jain, low-FODMAP, a religious fast), write it
in plain words in the feast sheet, ask the guest which dishes work, and add that dish to their
set-aside plate by hand.

## Flags

beef, pork, poultry, lamb, fish, shellfish, mollusc, egg, dairy, gluten, peanut, treenut,
sesame, soy, mustard, celery, lupin, sulphite, alcohol, honey, gelatin.

These cover the major allergen lists used on food labels in most regions plus common diet
items. Hidden sources to ask about: soy and wheat in sauces, sesame in dressings and buns, nuts
in pesto and satay, egg in glazes and mayonnaise, gelatin in sweets, dairy in "plain" bread,
alcohol in desserts.

## Questions for a severe allergy

1. Which foods exactly? Are traces ("may contain") a problem?
2. Does touching or breathing it matter (e.g. nut dust, frying oil shared with shellfish)?
3. Is medicine with them, and who knows how to use it?
4. Would they prefer to bring their own food? (A fine answer; still label the table.)

## On the table

- One label per dish: name, contains, may contain, suitable for.
- One serving spoon per dish; never move spoons between dishes.
- Put the allergy-safe and diet-safe dishes first in the line, or on a separate table.
- Set-aside plates are made up before anyone serves themselves, wrapped and named.

## Table clock

- Perishable food at room temperature: off the table within 2 hours, 1 hour above 32 C / 90 F.
- Hot holding 60 C / 140 F or above; cold holding 4 C / 40 F or below; check with a thermometer.
  Many cheap warmers only reach about 45-50 C, which is not hot holding.
- Use small platters and replace them; do not add fresh food to a used platter.
- Leftovers into shallow containers and the fridge before the off-by time; past it, throw away.
- Reheat leftovers until steaming hot all the way through (74 C / 165 F).

Shelf-stable items (sealed crisps, bread, whole fruit, canned drinks) are `hold: shelf` and have
no clock.
